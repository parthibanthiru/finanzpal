Savings account or fixed deposit: which fits you?

Both protect your money and pay interest — the difference is how quickly you can get at it again.

DL
David Lang
Updated October 2, 2026 · 5 min read

Savings accounts (Tagesgeld) and fixed deposits (Festgeld) are often mentioned in the same breath, but they work differently. Both are protected by statutory deposit protection up to €100,000 per customer and bank — the key difference is how available your money is.

1. The basic difference

With a savings account, your money stays available at any time — you can withdraw or deposit daily, with no notice period. With a fixed deposit, you lock in a set amount for an agreed term (e.g. 6, 12 or 24 months) and in return often get a somewhat higher, guaranteed rate.

GOOD TO KNOW

Savings-account rates are mostly variable and can change at any time. Fixed-deposit rates stay fixed for the whole term — in both directions: you don’t benefit from later rate rises, but you’re also protected from cuts.

2. A worked example

Say you have €10,000 available. With a savings account at a variable 3.0% rate, you’d earn around €300 interest in the first year, though the rate can change over the year. With a 12-month fixed deposit at a guaranteed 3.25% rate, you’re guaranteed €325 — but you can’t access the money easily before the 12 months are up.

3. How to decide

  • Emergency fund or money needed soon: Savings account, because of its constant availability.
  • Money you definitely won’t need for a while: Fixed deposit, if the guaranteed rate beats the current savings-account rate.
  • Not sure how long you want to invest it: A mix of both, or start with a savings account until you’re more certain.
