What to look for when choosing an ETF

These five metrics help you compare ETFs with a similar investment focus on a like-for-like basis.

TER (total expense ratio)

The annual fund cost as a percentage of fund assets. Even small differences add up noticeably over many years.

Replication

Physical means the ETF directly holds the index's securities. Synthetic uses a swap agreement to track the index return — common for money-market and commodity ETFs.

Distribution policy

Accumulating automatically reinvests dividends inside the fund; distributing pays them out to you regularly. For long-term wealth building, accumulating is often more practical.

Fund size

Larger funds are less likely to be closed or merged into another fund, which can spare you tax and administrative hassle.

Fund age

A longer track record shows how reliably the ETF has tracked its index across different market conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Why isn't this list complete?

A complete ETF market covers several thousand products with figures that change continuously, usually sourced from licensed financial-data feeds. Instead, we show a curated selection of well-known, established ETFs with data we consider reliable — a starting point for your own comparison, not a final market overview.

Which ETF is the "best"?

That depends on your investment strategy — time horizon, desired diversification and risk tolerance differ from person to person. That's why we deliberately don't show a single recommendation, just the figures to weigh up yourself.

Where do I buy an ETF?

Through a brokerage account with a broker or bank, either as a one-off purchase or a monthly savings plan. You can find a broker comparison on our Broker comparison page.

What does UCITS mean?

UCITS ETFs meet EU-wide standards on risk diversification and transparency and are the common fund type for retail investors in Germany. Almost all ETFs traded on German exchanges are UCITS-compliant.
