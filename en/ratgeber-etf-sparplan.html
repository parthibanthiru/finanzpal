Starting an ETF savings plan: first steps

What to know before your first order — from choosing a broker to the right savings rate, no jargon.

JM
Julia Mertens
Updated September 18, 2026 · 7 min read

An ETF savings plan is one of the simplest ways to build wealth long-term — you invest a fixed amount regularly, regardless of where the market stands. What matters most is getting the first decisions right, since they’re only corrected later with some effort.

1. Choosing the right broker

Not every broker offers every ETF as a savings plan, and fees differ noticeably. For most beginners, two things matter most: no or low custody fees, and a savings plan with no minimum above €25.

2. Finding the right ETF

For getting started, a single, broadly diversified ETF tracking a world index is usually enough. It covers hundreds or thousands of companies at once, which significantly reduces the risk of individual mistakes.

  • Broad diversification: A world ETF covers companies from many countries and sectors.
  • Low ongoing costs: Check the total expense ratio (TER) — under 0.3% is considered cheap.
  • Physical replication: The ETF actually buys the underlying stocks rather than just tracking them synthetically.
GOOD TO KNOW

A higher savings rate is almost always easier to adjust than a poorly chosen ETF. Better to start small and raise the rate later than to hesitate too long over the choice.

3. Setting the savings rate

There’s no “correct” amount — what matters is that you can sustain it long-term without financial strain. Many start with €50 to €100 a month and adjust the rate as their own financial situation changes. Our savings plan calculator shows you directly how different rates play out long-term.

The best savings plan is the one you don’t abandon even in a bad stock-market year.

4. Set it up and let it run

Once set up, the savings plan continues automatically — monthly or quarterly execution dates are typical. Checking in once a year is usually enough to confirm the ETF and rate still fit your situation.

Common mistakes when starting out

  1. Spreading across too many ETFs at once instead of starting with one broad core.
  2. Pausing the savings plan when prices fall instead of sticking with the rate.
  3. Overlooking custody fees that noticeably eat into returns over the years.
