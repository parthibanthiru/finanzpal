Why Finanzpal exists

Financial decisions are rarely complicated because the subject itself is complicated — usually it’s because offers are made hard to compare. Finanzpal translates fee structures, interest models and contract terms into comparisons you can understand in a few minutes.

We’re not a financial advisor and don’t replace individual advice. Our goal is that every recommendation stays traceable — with disclosed criteria instead of a black-box rating.

HOW FINANZPAL MAKES MONEY

If you choose a product via a link on Finanzpal, we often receive a commission from the provider. This costs you nothing extra — the price is the same one you’d pay without our link.

Commission amounts differ between providers. So that this doesn’t distort our rating, commission never decides the order in a comparison table — that’s governed solely by the criteria in our methodology below.

Our methodology by category

Each category has its own criteria, because brokers, instant savings accounts, loans and insurance can’t be compared by the same standards.

Broker

Account fee, ETF savings-plan offer, cash interest and minimum savings rate — see Finanzpal Score below.

Children's custody account

Account fee, minimum savings rate, cash interest and parent-account effort — see the Finanzpal Score below.

Checking account

Account fee, overdraft rate, cash interest, and card/withdrawal flexibility — see the Finanzpal Score below.

Instant savings account

Interest rate, rate-guarantee duration and deposit protection — see the Finanzpal Score below.

Credit card

Annual fee, worldwide withdrawal terms, whether it's condition-free, and interest-free days — see the Finanzpal Score below.

Loans

Effective annual rate, free overpayment rights and loan amount range — see the Finanzpal Score below.

Insurance — personal liability

Annual premium, rating confirmation (Franke & Bornberg PHV rating FFF+) and cover amount — see the Finanzpal Score below.

Insurance — home contents, disability, legal, car

Scope of cover, exclusions in the fine print, value for money, experiences from claims — no Finanzpal Score yet, see the reasoning below.

The Finanzpal Score

On some comparison pages we show a Finanzpal Score (0–100 plus a letter band A–D) alongside the raw data — this is our own, openly-published assessment, not an independent rating from an agency or regulator. It combines several criteria into one number, but only ever using figures that are already shown on that same page — no additional, invisible assumptions. The weighting is fixed and doesn't shift just because a provider is added or removed. Commissions never factor into the score — the same rule that already governs the order of our comparison tables.

Currently computed for: checking accounts (account fee 35% · overdraft rate 25% · cash interest 20% · card & cash-withdrawal flexibility 20%), loans (interest rate 55% · free overpayment rights 30% · loan amount range 15%), credit cards (annual fee 30% · worldwide withdrawal 30% · condition-free 20% · interest-free days 20%), instant savings accounts (interest rate 55% · rate-guarantee duration 20% · deposit protection 25%), children's custody accounts (account fee 20% · minimum savings rate 15% · cash interest 30% · parent-account effort 35%) and brokers & custody accounts (account fee 30% · ETF savings-plan offer 25% · cash interest 25% · minimum savings rate 20%) and personal liability insurance (annual premium 35% · rating confirmation 40% · cover amount 25%). For personal liability insurance, confirmation in the independent Franke & Bornberg 2026 PHV rating (top grade FFF+) carries the most weight, deliberately more than price alone — for a liability policy, a particularly cheap but not independently confirmed tariff shouldn't automatically outrank a confirmed one. A provider without that confirmation isn't scored as "bad", just "not independently confirmed" — a real, disclosed difference. Getsafe currently gets no score because it publishes no flat annual premium at all — tariffs are calculated fully digitally inside its own app, so there's simply no comparable premium figure. For brokers & custody accounts, two fields that are also shown on the page are deliberately left out of the score: the order fee, because several providers themselves state conflicting figures for it (different amounts depending on the source), and the star rating next to the provider name — that's our own editorial assessment, not separately documented elsewhere, and the score is meant as the disclosed alternative to it, not something blended together with it. For free overpayment rights, only terms the provider explicitly states as free count — a bare mention of "possible anytime" with no cost stated isn't automatically treated as free. When a credit card's interest-free day count isn't stated (common for debit or charge cards, which settle differently), we score that neutral rather than as a downside — a different card model isn't automatically a worse one. For deposit protection, a conversion the source itself flags as unclear (e.g. a foreign currency) only counts as partial credit, and an explicit note that part of a balance can be held without deposit protection at all weighs in accordingly — neither is silently scored as an unconditional guarantee. For children's custody accounts, whether a parent needs their own account at the same provider just to open one for their child carries the heaviest weight, since that's the factor that most sets this category apart from an ordinary adult custody account; cash interest flagged as a new-customer promotion is marked down slightly, since the provider itself says it can drop again after a set period. Categories without a reliable per-provider single price (mortgages, disability insurance, car insurance, legal protection) don't get a score yet on purpose — faking a precise number on top of data that's already flagged as uncertain would undercut exactly what the score is meant to do. For the same reason, home contents insurance also stays unscored (6 of 10 providers' premium field literally says the source itself didn't separately research a price for the test household), as do the other insurance tabs that share the same comparison page as personal liability. More categories will follow as their criteria are settled.
