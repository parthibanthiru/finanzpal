What to look for when choosing a children's custody account

A children's account differs from a regular adult custody account in a few important ways.

Does a parent need their own account?

Some brokers (e.g. Trade Republic, Scalable Capital, flatex) require both parents to already hold their own account there. Others (e.g. Consorsbank, comdirect) allow opening one independently of that.

Minimum savings rate

As with adult accounts, the smallest amount you can invest per execution varies — from €1 to €25, depending on the provider.

Transfer at adulthood

On the child's 18th birthday, the account automatically passes into their sole control — parents lose access from that point on.

Tax exemption allowance

Children have their own tax-free savings allowance (Sparerpauschbetrag). A separate exemption order (Freistellungsauftrag) for the children's account ensures capital gains stay tax-free up to that limit.

Frequently asked questions

Who owns the money in a children's custody account?

Legally, the account belongs to the child from the start. Parents manage it in trust until the child turns 18; after that, the child gets full control.

Do I need my own account with the same provider as a parent?

It depends on the provider. Trade Republic, Scalable Capital and flatex require both parents to have their own account. Consorsbank and comdirect currently don't.

What happens to the account when the child turns 18?

The account automatically continues in the name of the — now adult — child. Parents lose access, and the child can use it freely.
