Comparing instalment loans: finding the lowest rate

The nominal rate alone tells you little — the effective annual rate shows what a loan really costs.

DL
David Lang
Updated October 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Comparing loans, you quickly run into two different percentages: the nominal rate and the effective annual rate. Looking only at the lower number can mean comparing apples to oranges — and paying more than you expected in the end.

1. Nominal rate vs. effective annual rate

The nominal rate is the pure interest rate on the loan amount. The effective annual rate also factors in all other costs of the loan — such as processing fees — and the exact repayment schedule. German banks are legally required (Preisangabenverordnung) to state the effective annual rate in every advertisement, which makes it the only figure that's genuinely comparable between providers.

GOOD TO KNOW

Many banks advertise a particularly low "from" rate that only applies to customers with excellent creditworthiness. You usually only find out the rate that actually applies to you after a non-binding inquiry, which leaves no trace on your Schufa record.

2. A worked example

Say you finance €10,000 over 48 months at an effective annual rate of 5.9%. That works out to a monthly instalment of around €234 — over the full term you'd pay roughly €1,250 in interest on top of the loan amount. Even a one-percentage-point difference in rate can mean several hundred euros at this amount and term. Use our loan calculator to run the numbers for your own amount and term.

3. What else to watch for when comparing

  • Free early repayment: The option to pay back part or all of the loan early without an early-repayment penalty — saves real money if your financial situation improves.
  • Creditworthiness-dependent rate (2/3 rate): Providers must state the rate that at least two-thirds of their customers actually receive — a more realistic benchmark than the advertised "from" rate.
  • Term vs. monthly instalment: A longer term lowers the monthly payment but increases total interest — weigh what matters more for your budget and your overall cost.
  • Payment protection insurance: Often bundled in automatically, but it's optional and noticeably increases the cost of the loan — a separate, standalone comparison is usually worth more than the bundled version.
