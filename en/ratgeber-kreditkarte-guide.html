Free credit card: what to actually watch for

€0 annual fee sounds good — but the foreign transaction fee and withdrawal terms decide whether a card is really free.

JM
Julia Mertens
Updated October 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Almost every provider now advertises a credit card with no annual fee. That's often true — but "free" usually refers to just one single thing: the annual fee. What happens when you pay abroad or withdraw cash is a different matter.

1. What "free" actually means

Many cards stay permanently fee-free only under certain conditions: a minimum monthly deposit, a particular (often fee-based) current account with the same provider, or a minimum number of payments per month. If that condition stops being met, €0 can quickly turn into an annual fee.

GOOD TO KNOW

Check the conditions not just when you apply, but afterward too: some banks change them retroactively and only notify you by email or in your online banking inbox — easy to miss.

2. The two cost traps people often overlook

The real differences don't show up on the homepage — they're in the fee schedule:

  • Foreign transaction fee: A surcharge, often 1.5–2%, on every payment or withdrawal outside the eurozone. On a trip with €1,000 in spending, that quickly adds up to €15–20 — the best cards skip this entirely.
  • Withdrawal terms: Some cards allow only a limited number of free withdrawals per month, others only above a minimum amount or only at certain ATM operators. If you need cash often, check this carefully before signing up.

3. Do you need a credit card in addition to your checking account?

A debit card tied to your checking account is usually enough for everyday life in Germany. An additional credit card pays off mainly in these cases:

  • Travel and payments abroad: Credit cards are accepted at more places worldwide than debit cards.
  • Online bookings abroad: Car rentals and hotel bookings often require a credit card as a deposit or guarantee — a debit card is often not accepted.
  • Separate overview: If you want to bundle spending and settle it once a month, rather than having it deducted immediately from your checking account.
