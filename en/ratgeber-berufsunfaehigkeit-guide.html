Disability income insurance: do you really need it?

If illness stops you from working, state support is often minimal — disability income insurance (BU) closes exactly that gap.

JM
Julia Mertens
Updated October 20, 2026 · 6 min read

German consumer advocates consider Berufsunfähigkeitsversicherung (BU), disability income insurance, one of the most important policies a person can have — and at the same time one of the most commonly underestimated. The reason: statutory protection if you can no longer work falls far short for most people.

1. Why statutory protection often isn't enough

Anyone born after 1961 is no longer entitled to the classic statutory disability pension, only to the lower "Erwerbsminderungsrente" (reduced earning-capacity pension). That's only paid if you can no longer work in any job at all — not just the one you trained for. And even then, it's often well below your last net income. Self-employed people, students, and many civil servants often have no statutory entitlement at all.

GOOD TO KNOW

Only people born before 1961 are still entitled to the older, more generous disability pension. Everyone born after that falls solely under the lower reduced-earning-capacity pension — one reason private coverage matters even more for younger generations.

2. Who needs disability income insurance most

  • Early-career professionals: Premiums are usually noticeably cheaper when you're young, and health questions are often easier to answer.
  • Physically or psychologically demanding jobs: Manual, physical, or high-stress work carries a higher risk of becoming unable to work in your profession.
  • Sole earners and families: If the income disappears, the whole family's financial base disappears with it.
  • Self-employed people: No employer to keep paying during illness, and often no entitlement to the statutory reduced-earning-capacity pension either.

3. What matters when choosing a policy

The premium isn't the most important criterion for BU — the contract terms decide whether and how quickly you get paid if something happens:

  • No abstract referral clause ("Verzicht auf abstrakte Verweisung"): The insurer may not point you to some other, theoretically possible job you could still do instead of paying out.
  • Clear disability definition: Benefits should kick in from 50% disability — now industry standard, but not guaranteed on every policy.
  • Future-increase guarantee ("Nachversicherungsgarantie"): The option to raise your insured amount later without a new health check, e.g. after a raise, buying a house, or starting a family.
  • Term: The policy should run at least until your planned retirement age — a gap in your last working years is especially risky.
