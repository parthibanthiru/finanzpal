Tax filing software: is it worth it?

The tax office provides ELSTER, a free portal. Yet plenty of people still pay for tax software — here's why.

JM
Julia Mertens
Updated October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Every German tax return can be filed for free through ELSTER, the tax authorities' official online portal. Even so, many people turn to paid tax software or apps instead. The reason rarely has to do with the act of filing itself — it's about what happens before that.

1. What ELSTER does — and doesn't do

ELSTER accepts your entries and transmits them securely to the tax office. What it doesn't offer is a guided, plain-language walkthrough: its forms follow official tax-form logic, not your everyday life. If you're not familiar with terms like deductible work expenses, extraordinary burdens, or Anlage N, you're on your own to figure it out.

2. What tax software offers instead

Paid programs walk you through everyday-language questions ("Did you drive to work by car?") and automatically translate your answers into the right form fields. On top of that come plausibility checks that flag possible errors or deductions you might have missed. Across several providers compared, entry-level prices recently ranged from roughly €30 to €46 per tax year or filing, depending on whether it's an annual subscription or a single license.

GOOD TO KNOW

Whether the cost of software pays off depends heavily on your situation. For a simple employee tax return with no special cases, the difference versus ELSTER is often small. With rental income, self-employment, or several side jobs, though, good software can quickly surface more in extra refund than it costs.

3. What to look for when choosing

Not every program covers every case equally well — some are built for employees, others for rental income or self-employment. Filing also differs: some providers require your own ELSTER certificate, others handle that in the background. Our tax software comparison shows which provider fits your situation.
