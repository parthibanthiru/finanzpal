Retirement

The Altersvorsorgedepot: what changes in 2027

The Riester pension is being retired. Here's what's already confirmed about its replacement, what's still open — and what you can plan for today.

DL
David Lang
Updated October 21, 2026 · 7 min read

The Riester pension has long had a reputation problem: high costs, complicated subsidy rules, and a guarantee requirement that left little room for returns in a low-interest environment. From 1 January 2027, the Altersvorsorgedepot (AVD) is set to launch as its successor — allowing freer investing while keeping a government subsidy attached.

1. What the Altersvorsorgedepot changes

Unlike many existing Riester contracts, the AVD allows free, return-oriented investing — in ETFs or funds, for example — instead of a rigid guaranteed product. The government subsidy stays, but it's tied to the new product. If you already hold a Riester contract, you'll likely be able to keep it as-is or switch to the new product; the exact transition rules aren't fully settled yet at the time of writing.

2. The planned subsidy, in brief

A base subsidy of 50 cents per euro contributed, up to an annual cap, is planned, plus an additional 25 cents per euro for amounts above that up to a further limit. A cap on ongoing product costs of around 1% per year is also planned, so more of the subsidy and returns reach the saver. The exact amounts, thresholds, and conditions will only be final once the legislation is passed.

GOOD TO KNOW

Because details on the base subsidy, child subsidy, career-starter bonus, and taxation aren't finalized yet, there's no need to rush a decision. An existing Riester contract keeps running as normal in the meantime.

3. What you can already plan for today

Even without the final subsidy details, you can already run the pure savings and payout math: how much capital will you build by retirement, and what monthly pension could that support over a chosen payout period? Our retirement savings calculator models exactly that — deliberately without presenting the not-yet-finalized subsidy figures as precise numbers.
