Free checking account: what really matters

€0 account fees sound tempting — but conditions, overdraft rates, and card benefits decide whether an account is actually cheap in the end.

JM
Julia Mertens
Updated October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Almost every bank now advertises a "free" checking account. Look closer, though, and "free" is often tied to conditions — and the account fee itself is just one of several cost factors that add up over the years.

1. Account fees and their conditions

Many accounts stay fee-free only if you show a minimum monthly incoming payment (e.g. your salary) or are under a certain age. Some banks offer €0 only for a limited time for new customers, then charge a regular €4–5 a month once the condition no longer applies. It's worth checking the fine print carefully rather than relying on the word "free" in the ad alone.

2. Don't underestimate the overdraft rate

The interest rate for overdrafts varies substantially between banks — effective annual rates of roughly 7% to over 10% are common in the market. If you overdraw by a few hundred euros a month, that adds up over a year to far more than the account fee you saved by choosing a "free" account. If you regularly dip into the red, this rate deserves more weight than the account fee itself.

GOOD TO KNOW

A low overdraft rate doesn't help much if you never actually use it. Be honest about how often your account actually goes negative before weighting this criterion too heavily.

3. Cards and cash withdrawals

Whether a Visa or Mastercard debit card is included, how many withdrawals per month are fee-free, and whether that also applies abroad varies a lot between providers. If you travel often, look specifically for fee-free foreign-currency withdrawals — that saves more than a few euros of monthly account fees.
