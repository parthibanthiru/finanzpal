What to look for when choosing a custody account

These criteria help you find the right account for savings-plan investing.

ETF savings-plan selection

How many ETFs are available as a savings plan, and how many of them are fee-free? A large selection matters most if you want to invest in several ETFs at once.

Minimum savings rate

The smallest amount you can invest per savings-plan execution — important if you're starting small or want to split across several ETFs.

Interest on cash balance

Many brokers pay interest on uninvested cash in the settlement account — the rate and whether a cap applies varies a lot between providers.

Account and custody fees

Most modern custody accounts are free in principle, but some providers charge a fee that's only waived under certain conditions.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between your broker and custody-account comparisons?

The broker comparison contrasts order and account fees for active trading. This custody-account comparison looks specifically at the savings-plan perspective: ETF selection, minimum savings rate, execution intervals and cash interest — relevant if you invest regularly and long-term rather than placing individual trades.

Why do some custody accounts pay interest on my balance and others don't?

Brokers that run their own cash or settlement account can often pay a more attractive rate on uninvested money than traditional banks. For some providers, the rate isn't clearly disclosed, or is only available as a limited-time new-customer promotion.

Is a custody account with a fee worth it?

Only if the conditions for waiving it (e.g. an active savings plan, a minimum number of trades) are met anyway, or additional services justify the cost. For pure savings-plan investing, there are several permanently free alternatives.
