How is this calculated?

The monthly payment is loan amount × (interest rate + initial repayment rate) ÷ 12. Each month, the payment splits into an interest share and a principal share; as the remaining balance falls, the interest share shrinks and the principal share grows accordingly. Once the fixed-rate period ends, the remaining balance in practice needs follow-up financing at whatever rate applies then, which can be higher or lower than the original rate.

Frequently asked questions

What happens after the fixed-rate period ends?

The remaining balance needs to be refinanced at whatever market rate applies then (follow-up financing or a switch to a new lender) — the new rate could be higher or lower than the original one.

How does a higher repayment rate affect things?

A higher initial repayment rate increases the monthly payment but significantly lowers the remaining balance at the end of the fixed period, and saves interest over the full term.

What's a sensible fixed-rate period?

When rates are low, a long fixed period (15–20 years) offers planning certainty. When rates are high, some people deliberately choose a shorter fixed period to benefit if rates fall later — but that's a bet on how rates will move.
